# Collecting

*The operating guide for Why Today?'s first phase of editorial work: noticing, capturing, and following threads. This document governs the cabinet of curiosities before anything becomes an edition.*

---

## The Core Distinction

A **story** is a unit optimized for news. It has a dateline, a subject, a consequence.

A **curiosity** is a unit optimized for investigation. It begins with an observation or question and may go anywhere.

Why Today? has always been built on curiosities — the editions that have worked were never really about the news events that opened them. July 4th was a curiosity about how national memory is constructed. The heat dome was a curiosity about why invisible deaths don't generate the same response as visible ones. The penalty kick was a curiosity about why human beings cannot do the optimal thing when it matters most.

The mistake was collecting stories and then trying to find the curiosity inside them. The better direction is to collect curiosities directly — and let news remain one source among many.

---

## The Editorial Objective

The system should help you:

1. Notice something
2. Capture the observation before trying to explain it
3. Generate better questions around it
4. Follow promising threads with AI-assisted research
5. Identify the deeper relationship or perceptual shift
6. Eventually turn the strongest discoveries into Why Today? editions

Optimize for curiosity and perception, not publishing volume.

---

## The Five Collecting Streams

The cabinet of curiosities is organized around five streams. News is one stream, not the primary frame.

### 1. Natural World

The living world, behaving.

Examples:
- Seasonal produce arriving (stone fruit, tomatoes, figs)
- Tides, currents, ocean temperature
- Flowers blooming or going to seed
- Bird migration or unusual behavior
- Insects appearing or disappearing
- Moon phases and their effects on behavior
- Stars, planets, meteor showers
- Weather patterns (fog, heat, the first cold morning)
- Changes in light quality across the season
- Sounds that belong to a particular time of year

The collecting question: *What is the living world doing right now that most people are walking past?*

### 2. Human Rituals

The shared behaviors that millions of people perform quietly, without announcement.

Examples:
- School beginning or ending
- Summer grilling
- Farmers markets at their peak
- People buying peaches or corn or apples
- Commuting patterns changing seasonally
- Opening-day traditions for sports, fairs, hunting seasons
- Evening walks in summer heat
- Holiday preparation (the early signs)
- Seasonal foods appearing in kitchens
- The habits that define a season without being named as such

The collecting question: *What is everyone doing this week that no one is talking about?*

### 3. Places

A specific place, doing something interesting.

Examples:
- Neighborhoods
- Coastlines and their seasonal behavior
- Parks, plazas, public spaces
- Train stations and airports
- Cafés, markets, local businesses
- Streets at a particular time of day
- Places that transform in a specific season or at a specific hour

The important question is not: *What is this place?*

It is: *What does this place teach, reward, reveal, or make possible?*

Shade falling at the same time each afternoon is more interesting than a description of a park. What happens to a street when the school lets out is more interesting than the street itself.

### 4. Curiosities

Observations or questions that branch outward through investigation.

Examples:
- Why do waves arrive in sets?
- Why does a tomato stem smell so strongly?
- Why do old neighborhoods feel different from new ones?
- Why do surfers check the ocean even when they're not surfing?
- Why does fog change the apparent distance of sound?
- Why do certain foods taste different at different times of year?
- Why does the same commute feel different in different seasons?

A curiosity may begin with something overheard, something noticed, something read, something photographed, or something that didn't have an explanation when it happened.

The collecting question: *What am I not explaining to myself, and why is that interesting?*

### 5. Headlines

Timely news, treated as one entry point among several — not the primary frame.

The existing news collection continues. The six categories (Politics & World, Business & Markets, Tech & Science, Sports, Culture & Entertainment, Odd & Human Interest) remain valid. What changes is the role of headlines in the system: they are one stream, not the definition of what "today" means.

A headline is worth collecting when it contains a curiosity — when there is something underneath it that the headline doesn't reach. A headline that is merely important, or merely timely, without an underlying question, is not a strong candidate for an edition.

The editorial conversion: when a headline arrives, the instinct should be to ask *what is the observation or question hiding inside this?* rather than *how important is this story?*

A meteor-shower headline is not interesting as an astronomy item. It becomes interesting when it produces this: *Tonight, Earth is passing through a trail of ancient dust. What changes when you realize a meteor shower is something the planet is moving through, not something happening to the sky?*

## The Curiosity Schema

### Essential Capture Fields

These two fields are the minimum viable capture. Everything else can develop later.

**Observation:** I noticed…

**Question:** I wonder…

A curiosity captured in under a minute with just these two fields is more valuable than a longer entry that wasn't captured at all.

### Extended Fields

These can be filled in immediately, during investigation, or not at all. They exist to serve the work, not to impose structure.

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| `stream` | Which of the five streams: `natural_world`, `human_rituals`, `places`, `curiosities`, `headlines` |
| `title` | Short label for the curiosity |
| `observation` | "I noticed…" — the raw capture |
| `question` | "I wonder…" — the opening question |
| `hidden_question` | The editorial question as it sharpens through investigation |
| `status` | Where this curiosity sits in the lifecycle |
| `editorial_potential` | `high`, `medium`, or `low` |
| `location` | For place-based curiosities |
| `season_or_time_context` | The seasonal or temporal anchor |
| `why_now` | Why this is worth capturing today |
| `sensory_details` | What you would smell, see, hear, touch |
| `possible_connections` | What else this might connect to |
| `ai_questions` | Questions the AI surfaced that are worth following |
| `research_notes` | What investigation has found so far |
| `emerging_insight` | The thread that is beginning to resolve |
| `perceptual_shift` | The changed way of seeing this produces |
| `reader_gift` | What the reader takes into the world |
| `next_step` | The immediate next action on this curiosity |
| `url` | Source link, if applicable |
| `captured_at` / `first_seen` | When this entered the cabinet |

Do not fill in all fields for every curiosity. Most curiosities should be allowed to remain incomplete. The extended fields are available when the work wants them.

## Status Lifecycle

A curiosity moves through the following stages. Most observations will never leave **Captured** or **Curious** — that is fine.

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Captured** | Noticed and recorded. No commitment to investigate. |
| **Curious** | Returned to this; the question has some energy. AI may have been asked to expand it. |
| **Investigating** | Actively following threads. Research is in progress. |
| **Thread Found** | A specific angle or surprising sentence has emerged. |
| **Ready to Shape** | Enough is known to begin structuring an edition. |
| **Published** | Became a Why Today? edition. |
| **Dormant** | Set aside. May become relevant months or seasons later. Remains searchable. |

The status workflow is not a pipeline that everything must move through. It is a vocabulary for describing where a curiosity currently sits.

A curiosity that stays at **Dormant** for a year and then becomes relevant because of a seasonal event is behaving correctly.

A curiosity that moves from **Captured** to **Published** in a week is also behaving correctly.

There is no required cadence.

## What AI Should Do in the Collecting Phase

The AI should not primarily ask: *Is this a strong news story?*

Instead, the AI should help answer:

- **What is interesting here that I may not yet see?** What is the hidden strangeness in this ordinary thing?
- **What questions am I not asking?** What angle hasn't been tried?
- **What larger systems, rituals, histories, sciences, or relationships might connect to this?** What is the web this observation lives in?
- **What would different people notice here?** A farmer, naturalist, historian, scientist, chef, architect, child, local resident, or craftsperson would see different things in the same observation.
- **Is there a sensory or practical way for a reader to experience this themselves?** Can the reader verify or encounter this in their own life?
- **What is the smallest insight that could permanently change how someone encounters this ordinary thing?** Not the largest or most profound. The smallest durable one.
- **Is there a genuine thread here, or are we forcing meaning onto it?** The most important question to ask before investing in investigation.

### What AI Should Avoid

- Generic philosophical conclusions that could attach to any subject
- Sentimental language that feels more meaningful than it is
- Forced life lessons (the lesson that arrives before the investigation is not a discovery)
- Overclaiming the significance of early-stage observations
- Turning every curiosity toward publication pressure

The strongest outcomes may be very small:

*Smell the stem before buying a tomato.*

*Look at the horizon before checking the surf forecast.*

*Notice how fog changes the apparent distance of sound.*

*Watch where shade falls at the same time each afternoon.*

These are not failed insights waiting to become something grander. They are the gift.

## The Editorial Test

For any curiosity that advances toward an edition, the system should help evaluate one question:

**What will the reader notice differently tomorrow because of this?**

This must be answered concretely — not "they will think more deeply about X" but "they will notice the tomato stem, or look at the horizon, or hear the fog differently."

This does not mean every edition must be profound. The goal is enrichment: making an ordinary day feel slightly deeper, more legible, or more alive.

The story is the opening.
The investigation follows the thread.
The final gift is the changed perception.

## Scoring and Ranking

The existing `editorial_potential` field (high/medium/low) remains the primary signal. It is deliberately coarse. Numerical precision in editorial scoring is false confidence.

**Signals that increase potential:**
- A clear observation that precedes explanation
- A genuinely unanswered question (not rhetorical)
- Sensory richness — something a reader can smell, feel, or hear
- The presence of an unexpected relationship between two things
- The possibility of a concrete perceptual shift
- Something the reader can experience themselves
- Seasonal or place-based relevance (things happening *now*, not just *always*)
- The quality of lingering — does this come back?

**Signals that decrease potential:**
- Importance without strangeness
- A question that has a clean Wikipedia answer
- A topic that requires domain expertise to appreciate
- A subject that is inherently partisan
- Something that will feel dated in three weeks

**Signals to reduce in weight from the previous news-centered model:**
- Breaking news intensity
- Virality and broad social reach
- Publisher prominence
- Headline strength as a proxy for editorial strength
- Recency as a sufficient reason to publish

A story can be important, timely, and widely covered — and still contain no curiosity worth a Why Today? edition.

## Pattern Recognition

Over time, the cabinet should help reveal your editorial taste. Not by imposing categories but by surfacing what keeps appearing.

Recurring interests to watch for:

- Seasonality — things that happen at this exact time of year
- Coastlines and water
- Food and its making
- Neighborhoods and the life of streets
- Rituals and shared behavior
- Light and how it changes
- Weather as experience, not forecast
- Craft — how things are made
- Transitions (what changes when something ends or begins)
- Local economies and the people inside them
- Sensory details that teach something
- The relationship between people and place

These patterns should emerge from the collection rather than being imposed in advance. When they start to appear, note them. They are the publication's editorial fingerprint.

## Daily and Weekly Cadence

### Daily

Capture 1–5 observations with minimal friction. No requirement to research, explain, or publish. The essential act is noticing and recording before the observation dissolves.

The essential capture:
- *I noticed…*
- *I wonder…*

### Weekly

Review the cabinet. The question is not "what should I publish?" It is:

- What is still lingering from last week?
- Which observations are beginning to connect?
- Which question has energy — the kind that makes me want to follow a thread?
- Is there one curiosity that deserves deeper investigation?

Choose no more than one primary investigation at a time.

### During Investigation

Use AI to branch the questions, discover sources, test connections, challenge assumptions, and identify the strongest thread. The goal is not to confirm what you already think — it is to find what you didn't expect.

### Publication

Publish only when there is a clear opening (the shared moment or observation), a meaningful investigation (the thread and what it revealed), and a concrete reader gift (what changes tomorrow).

Do not optimize the system for producing editions at a fixed cadence. Optimize it for steadily improving your ability to notice, investigate, and point.

Most curiosities should be allowed to remain permanently unfinished. Dormant entries may become relevant in a different season, after a different experience, in the context of a different edition.

## What This Document Does Not Govern

The editorial algorithms for selecting questions and constructing editions remain in `system/ALGORITHM.md`. The heuristics for evaluating hidden questions remain in `system/HEURISTICS.md`. The patterns and failure modes remain in their respective documents.
